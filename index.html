About

I'm a consultant psychiatrist in Melbourne. My main clinical and research interest is neuropsychiatry: the overlap between mental illness and conditions affecting the brain. I hold RANZCP advanced certificates in old age psychiatry and consultation-liaison psychiatry.

Much of my work is with people whose symptoms sit between psychiatry and neurology: changes in mood, behaviour or thinking that could be a mental illness, an early dementia, or both. Telling these apart matters, because the treatment and support people need can be very different.

Alongside clinical work, I'm completing a PhD at the University of Melbourne, and I teach medical students and psychiatry trainees.

Research

Many conditions affecting behaviour, mood, and thinking look similar in their early stages. Someone in their fifties with new depression and a change in personality might have a primary psychiatric illness, or the first signs of a dementia. Our own research found that reaching the right diagnosis can take years.

I'm an investigator on the Markers in Neuropsychiatric Disorders (MiND) Study, and my PhD examines blood-based biomarkers in mood and anxiety disorders. I also work on clinical trials of new treatments for young-onset dementia.

My current research looks at how new blood tests for dementia, such as p-tau217 (a marker of Alzheimer's disease) and NfL (a marker of nerve-cell injury), behave in people with severe mood disorders, so clinicians can interpret the results accurately.
